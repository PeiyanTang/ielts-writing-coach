# Academic Writing Task 2

Identify the exact question type and every instruction: opinion, discussion, advantages/disadvantages, causes/solutions, two-part questions, or a hybrid. Labels are secondary to answering all prompt parts.

## Task Response checks

- Is every part of the prompt appropriately addressed?
- Is the writer's position clear, directly relevant, and consistent throughout?
- Does each body paragraph advance a distinct main idea?
- Is each idea extended through a defensible reasoning chain rather than assertion, repetition, or vague generalisation?
- Does support explain how or why the claim holds? Examples may be hypothetical but must be plausible and relevant.
- Are concessions and opposing views used purposefully rather than allowed to blur the position?

Preserve the learner's high-level stance when workable. The model may use stronger main ideas than the learner. When recommending a stance change, explain why the original direction creates a concrete task-response problem.

## Model answer

- Directly answer the exact question and maintain a clear position.
- Prefer two well-developed body ideas over a catalogue of shallow claims.
- Use natural academic English, precise vocabulary, varied controlled syntax, and unobtrusive cohesion.
- Avoid memorised openings, inflated claims, fabricated statistics presented as facts, and gratuitously rare words.
- Target a practical exam-length response, normally around 270-330 words unless the task calls for a modest variation.
